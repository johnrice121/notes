# Faith, Dignity, and Human Flourishing: Hearing God's Voice in an Age of AI

[← Back to Index](./index.md)

Last updated: 2026-06-24

## Context

Notes captured from Elder Gerrit W. Gong's six-part gospel workshop, "Faith, Dignity, and Human Flourishing:
Hearing God's Voice in an Age of Artificial Intelligence" (published June 2026).
[Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ts5Z64A0Vv4).

The talk sits squarely at this repo's intersection of AI, ethics, and meaning. Its central claim: AI is a tool that
should support, not supplant, revelation, judgment, and authentic relationship. "AI can answer questions, but it
cannot answer prayers." Captured here for the conclusion's practical invitations and for the many sources Elder Gong
cites, gathered as threads to explore later.

---

## The Frame

The workshop walks four relationships AI touches: **Thou** (God), **I** (self), **They** (others), and **It** (the
natural world), and offers three guideposts for personal AI use:

1. **Rely on the Spirit:** let technology support, not supplant, revelation, personal study, and covenant living.
2. **Practice wisdom:** apply judgment informed by doctrine and lived experience.
3. **Choose trusted sources:** anchor understanding in scripture, prophetic counsel, and reliable information.

Episodes (chapter markers):

- Opening (0:00)
- I: An Issue Defining Our Day (0:50)
- II: AI and Our Relationship with God / Thou (11:59)
- III: AI and Our Relationship with Self / I (22:32)
- IV: AI and Our Relationship with Others / They (31:45)
- V: AI and Our Relationship with the Environment and Natural World / It (38:08)
- VI: Concluding Thoughts on Hearing God's Voice in an Age of AI (52:50)

---

## Conclusion (Episode VI, ~52:50 on)

AI is a tool; the three guideposts keep it serving the four relationships rather than displacing them. Two threads
carry the close:

**The four invitations:**

1. **Set guidelines** for your AI use so it never gets between you and your relationship with God.
2. **Write down three truths about who you are:** "a child of God, a child of the covenant, and a disciple of Jesus
   Christ," and place them somewhere visible to pause and remember.
3. **Reach out personally:** send one message you write yourself, or visit a family member, friend, or neighbor in a
   meaningful way.
4. **Spend meaningful time in nature:** silence notifications and offer a prayer of gratitude.

**The "gift of possibility" turn:** rather than settle for "do not..." guardrails, aim AI at an "AI gift of human
possibility" that expands agency and dignity, prioritizes learning, and enlarges character. "I want AI which inspires
and enables anyone, anywhere, to have the possibility to become anything." He closes with the island of Nevis, the
birthplace of Alexander Hamilton, and a schoolteacher there who believes any of his students could be the next
Hamilton: "Anyone, anywhere, has the possibility to become anything."

### Interfaith and Philosophical

- [Antiqua et Nova][antiqua]: the Vatican's note on AI and human intelligence ("ancient and new," 2025)
- A Jewish chief rabbi's reading of the Midrash on Babel: men "wept more when clay bricks were broken than when
  human beings ... died" (often traced to Rabbi Jonathan Sacks; worth confirming)
- The Islamic concept of the "Adamic Covenant": the vertical bond with God and horizontal bond among people
- [Ubuntu][wiki-ubuntu] (Southern African): "I am because we are"

### Places and Cultural

## Follow-up and Reflection

Elder Gong's four closing invitations, kept here as personal follow-through:

1. **Set guidelines:** What explicit boundaries will I set so AI does not get between me and revelation, study, and
   prayer?
2. **Three truths:** Where will I place "a child of God, a child of the covenant, a disciple of Jesus Christ" so I see
   them daily and pause to remember?
3. **Reach out:** Who is the one person I will write in my own words, or visit meaningfully, this week?
4. **Time in nature:** When this week will I silence notifications and spend deliberate, grateful time outdoors?

A research thread: work through the sources above and follow up on those worth a full read. All of them could merit
it.
